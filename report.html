2. Method

The core test is Mosca's inequality: if the years the data must stay secret plus the years to migrate the system exceed the years until a quantum computer can break the algorithm, the system is already too late. The deadline is {{ horizon }} years for RSA, ECC and Diffie-Hellman (broken by Shor's algorithm at any key size), and zero for algorithms already broken today such as 3DES and SHA-1.

Each system is then scored out of 100: past the deadline ({{ weights.gap }}), quantum weakness of the algorithm ({{ weights.weakness }}), exposure of its traffic to copying ({{ weights.exposure }}) and impact, from data sensitivity and system criticality ({{ weights.impact }}). 75 and above is Critical, 55 High, 35 Medium. Because the arrival date of a quantum computer is unknown, the audit is also run at 7 and 15 years.

Migration targets are the NIST post-quantum standards: ML-KEM (FIPS 203) for key exchange, ML-DSA (FIPS 204) and SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) for signatures, deployed in hybrid mode first, and AES-256 for stored data.
